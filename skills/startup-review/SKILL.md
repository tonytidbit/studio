# Startup Review

## Instructions

Use the ten-slide framework as the guiding star. Treat each slide's subject as a
question the founder must be able to answer as if asked by a potential
stakeholder; this does not require creating a pitch deck.

Use the Business Model Canvas to understand and work with the startup or
project's foundation.

Use the summaries below for routine work. If the request goes beyond them,
source fidelity matters, or the agent is uncertain or corrected, consult only
the relevant linked source before continuing. Use it to improve the current
analysis rather than reproduce its wording; if access is unavailable, state the
limitation and ask focused questions.

When creating a new project that has not been identified as a startup, ask the
user whether this skill should apply before using it.

Distinguish documented evidence, reasonable inference, assumptions, and
unknowns. Do not invent answers for the founder.

## Starting from Zero

When no startup or project foundation exists yet, use the frameworks as guided
discovery rather than presenting a full review. Begin with:

- the problem or opportunity, intended customer, and proposed value
- how the founder will talk to potential users to validate the problem, intended
  customer, and proposed value before building
- whether the founder is already established in or connected to the target
  market
- the founder's current go-to-market strategy or understanding of how to reach
  the target market

Ask a small number of plain-language questions at a time. Preserve unknowns and
develop the remaining framework only as evidence and decisions emerge.

## Guy Kawasaki's Ten Slides

Source: https://guykawasaki.com/the-only-10-slides-you-need-in-your-pitch/
Source text license: All rights reserved

1. **Title:** Who are you, what is the startup or project, and how can you be
   reached?
2. **Problem or opportunity:** What important problem or opportunity are you
   addressing?
3. **Value proposition:** What value do you create, for whom, and why does it
   matter?
4. **Underlying magic:** How does the solution work, and what makes the approach
   meaningfully different?
5. **Business model:** Who pays, for what, and how does the startup make money?
6. **Go-to-market plan:** How will you reach, acquire, and serve customers?
7. **Competitive analysis:** What alternatives exist, how does this approach
   differ, and why would the intended customer choose it?
8. **Management team:** Who can execute this, and what relevant capabilities or
   gaps exist?
9. **Financial projections and key metrics:** Which projections and metrics
   describe progress and viability, and which assumptions do they test?
10. **Current status, accomplishments, timeline, and use of funds:** What has
    been achieved, what happens next, what funding or resources are needed, and
    how will they be used?

## Business Model Canvas

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_model_canvas
Source text license: [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)

1. **Customer segments:** Who is served?
2. **Value propositions:** What valuable outcomes are offered to each segment?
3. **Channels:** How are customers reached and how is value delivered?
4. **Customer relationships:** What relationship does each segment expect or
   require?
5. **Revenue streams:** Who pays, what do they pay for, and how?
6. **Key resources:** What assets, capabilities, data, people, or infrastructure
   are essential?
7. **Key activities:** What must the startup do especially well?
8. **Key partnerships:** Which external relationships are necessary or
   strategically valuable?
9. **Cost structure:** What are the material costs and economic constraints?

Treat the canvas as a dated working model rather than permanent truth.

## Review Structure

For a full startup review:

1. State the review scope, date, and evidence examined.
2. Review all ten stakeholder questions in their listed order.
3. Review all nine Business Model Canvas blocks in their listed order.
4. For each item, classify the current answer as:
   - established
   - partial
   - assumed
   - unknown
5. Cite or briefly identify the supporting evidence.
6. Reconcile the two frameworks by identifying:
   - strongest established foundations
   - contradictions
   - unsupported assumptions
   - consequential unknowns
7. Finish with focused questions and prioritized next decisions.

Do not replace this coverage with a generic findings list. When the user
requests a brief review, use a compact table or matrix, but account for every
item.

## Finish Up

Reconcile the stakeholder answers with the business-model foundation. Identify
contradictions, unsupported assumptions, unanswered questions, and the strongest
established answers.

Ask focused questions where the user can resolve important gaps. Finish with a
concise summary of the current position and the next decisions or validations
that matter most. Keep the depth proportional to the user's request.
